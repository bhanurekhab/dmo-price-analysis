# Default Market Offer — Eight Years of Prices in Real Terms

Independent analysis of the Australian Energy Regulator's Default Market Offer (DMO), covering eight years of annual determinations from DMO 1 (2019–20) through DMO 8 (2026–27).

**Date:** October 2026

## Overview

The Default Market Offer sets annual price caps for electricity customers on standing offers across five distribution zones in NSW, South East Queensland and South Australia. This project analyses pricing trends, cost component shares, and standing offer customer behaviour across the full eight-year history of the DMO. Both nominal and CPI-adjusted (real, 2019–20 dollars) price series are provided.

## How to run

**Requirements:** Python 3.8+

```bash
cd dmo-price-analysis
python scripts/analyse_dmo.py
```

This reads `data/dmo_prices.json` and produces all outputs in `docs/`, including CSV datasets, JSON files, and the interactive HTML report.

The HTML report (`docs/index.html`) is generated programmatically — all findings, data tables, and chart data are computed from `dmo_prices.json` and injected into the report template. Chart.js is embedded inline, so no internet connection is needed to view the report.

## Key findings

1. **The DMO protects a steadily shrinking group of customers** — Residential standing offer customers fell from 580,519 (10.4%) at DMO 4 to 475,160 (8.0%) at DMO 8 — an 18.1% reduction. Numbers were essentially flat between DMO 7 and DMO 8, but the regional picture diverges: residential NSW fell 10.4% while SE Queensland rose 16.9% and SA rose 8.9%. Small business standing offer customers fell 24.5% over the full period.

2. **In real terms the DMO has gone nowhere in eight years** — Ausgrid residential nominal prices are up 29.4% from DMO 1 to DMO 8 ($1,467 to $1,899), but in real terms (2019–20 dollars) the increase is just 1.0% ($1,467 to $1,482). The real series dipped below its starting point in 2021–22 and 2022–23 ($1,346 and $1,377), spiked with the 2023–24 increase driven by wholesale costs ([DMO 5 determination](https://www.aer.gov.au/system/files/Default%20market%20offer%20prices%202023-24%20final%20determination.pdf), s 1.2), and has now returned to roughly where it began. DMO 8 itself pulls back residential prices in four of five zones — the 3.4% nominal fall is a 7.0% real fall once inflation is stripped out. The AER attributes lower wholesale costs to falling contract prices for 2026–27 amid low spot price volatility (s 1.2).

3. **Regional divergence in DMO 8** — Energex (SE QLD) sees the largest residential flat rate decrease at –7.2%. SA Power Networks is the only zone with a flat rate price increase (+1.4% for residential). The AER says South Australian wholesale costs fell less than in other regions because of high-priced FCAS events in July and August 2025 (s 1.2). Network charges also mostly increased.

4. **Small business customers see larger price relief** — Small business DMO flat rate prices fall by an unweighted average of 9.3% across all five zones in DMO 8 (with a customer-weighted average also in the range of 9–10%), compared with an unweighted average decrease of 3.5% for residential, where SA Power Networks rose 1.4%.

5. **Network and wholesale charges are the two largest DMO components** — In DMO 8, network costs account for 39–54% of the price across zones, and wholesale costs represent 30–41%. Environmental schemes account for 2–3%, and retail costs 7–16%.

## Notes

- All prices are nominal annual reference prices for the flat rate (anytime) tariff, inclusive of GST
- Real (CPI-adjusted) prices are deflated to 2019–20 dollars using the ABS Consumer Price Index (cat. 6401.0), All Groups Weighted Average of Eight Capital Cities. The June quarter CPI at the start of each DMO financial year is used as the deflator, with Jun 2019 = 100
- Customer-weighted averages use approximate DNSP customer counts sourced from regulatory proposals, annual reports and AER jurisdictional snapshots (2023–25 vintage). These are total customer numbers, not split by tariff type
- Usage assumptions are zone-specific and set by the AER for a representative customer; current assumptions shown — DMO 5 (2023–24) used a 366-day year, giving slightly higher usage figures across all zones (e.g. 3,911 kWh for Ausgrid residential vs the standard 3,900 kWh; 10,027 kWh for small business vs 10,000 kWh). This inflates 2023–24 year-on-year changes by about 0.2–0.3 percentage points (estimated) and deflates 2024–25 changes by the same amount
- Small business usage assumption changed from 20,000 kWh to 10,000 kWh at DMO 4 (2022–23); earlier SB prices are excluded
- Cost component shares are AER-published ranges that depend on the region, not point estimates; the AER does not break them down by zone. Because each component's range spans different regions, the four ranges do not sum to 100%
- Standing offer customer data is drawn from AER determination Table 3.1 (DMO 4 through DMO 8). The determination tables themselves draw on the AER's quarterly retail market performance updates; the DMO 7 table uses the Q2 2024–25 update and DMO 8 uses the Q2 2025–26 update
- SE Queensland standing offer figures are approximate — extrapolated from all of Queensland by excluding Ergon Energy customers (per Table 3.1 notes in the AER determinations)
- DMO 6 (2024–25) prices reflect the AER's revised final determination
- DMO 8 applies a network tariff blending method for determining representative network costs (s 1.3.2)
